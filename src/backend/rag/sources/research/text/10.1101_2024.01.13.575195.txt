Genome sequence resources from three isolates of the apple canker pathogen Neonectria ditissima infecting forest trees.

Neonectria ditissima is a generalist ascomycete plant pathogen causing canker diseases on a variety of hardwood tree species and can cross-infect many of them. The fungus enters the plants through wounds throughout the year. N. ditissima is considered a major threat to apple production responsible for the fruit tree canker disease which damages trees and causes rotting of fruits in storage. Nearby forests and shelter belts can serve as source of inoculum for well-managed apple orchards. Thus, knowledge about the N. ditissima isolates infecting different host species is essential for designing integrated pest management strategies. Here, we describe the genomes of three N. ditissima isolates, Nd_iso34, Nd_iso35, and Nd_iso36, infecting European beech, American tulip tree, and American beech, respectively. We obtained genome assemblies of ca. 45 megabases for all isolates, covering 94% of the N. ditissima reference annotation, and 97% of the universal single-copy orthologs (BUSCOs). We conclude that these genome assemblies are a highly relevant resource considering the scarcity of genomic data available for N. ditissima.

The fungus, Neonectria ditissima (formerly N. galligena) causes a disease known as European 46 
canker or fruit tree canker, which is a serious threat to apple production in Sweden and globally 47 
(Weber, 2014; Garkava-Gustavsson et al., 201 6). N. ditissima is a wound pathogen, able to 48 
infect trees throughout the year and frui ts in orchards and storage ( Weber, 2014; Holthusen 49 
and Weber, 2021). The fungus has a wide range of hosts (Flack and Swinburne, 1977; Walter 50 
et al., 2015). N. ditissima can attack other fruit trees such as pear (Pyrus communis) and quince 51 
(Cydonia oblonga) (Walter et al., 2015) as well as many different species of forest trees , e.g. 52 
Acer, Betula, Fagus, Populus, Quercus and Salix (Ward et al., 2010).  Canker damages caused 53 
by N. ditissima have been recorded on forest trees in Europe  including Austria, Germany, and 54 
Slovakia (Cech, 2010; Metzler et al. 2002; Mihál, 2011). In North America, N. ditissima is 55 
known to be associated with Beach Bark Disease  but was also found on yellow birch (Betula 56 
alleghaniensis), stripped maple ( Acer pensylvanicum) and red maple ( Acer rubrum) (Kasson 57 
and Livingston, 2009). Hence, N. ditissima also has the propensity to cause damage to Swedish 58 
and European forestry through the extensive scarring and damage that leads to reductions in 59 
quality and value of timber (Metzler et al., 2002).  60 
 61 
In this study , we sequenced three isolates, Nd_iso34, Nd_iso35, and Nd_iso36, infecting 62 
European beech (Fagus sylvatica), American tulip tree (Liriodendron tulipifera), and American 63 
beech (Fagus grandifolia ), respectively. Fungal cultures were obtained from The 64 
Centraalbureau voor Schimmelcultures (CBS) and the Fungal Biodiversity Centre  (Royal 65 
Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences, KNAW). Nd_iso34 (syn. CBS 118920, AR 3690, 66 
BPI 870951 ) was isolated in 2001  from European beech  in Stiavnickey vrchy  (Slovakia). 67 
Nd_iso35 (syn. CBS 118919, GJS 04-350, BPI 864075) was isolated in 2004 from American 68 
tulip tree in the Great Smokey Mountains national park (Tennessee, USA). Finally, Nd_iso36 69 
(syn. CBS 118923, AR 4196, BPI 870950) was isolated in 2005 from American beech near Bass 70 
Lake (Michigan, USA).  71

High molecular weight genomic DNA was extracted from mycelium using the QIAGEN 73 
Genomic-tips according to the instructions from the manufacturer (QIAGEN, The 74 
Netherlands). DNA concentration and integrity were evaluated with the Qubit dsDNA 75 
Quantification Assay Kits  according to the manufacturer ( Invitrogen, USA), and further 76 
evaluated on 0,8 % agarose gel with Tris-acetate- Ethylenediaminetetraacetic Acid (TAE) buffer 77 
with DNA quantification standard. Sequencing libraries were prepared from 1µg DNA using 78 
the TruSeq PCRfree DNA sample preparation kit (cat# FC -121-3001/3002, Illumina Inc.) 79

manufacturers’ instructions (guide#15036187). Libraries were then applied to  150bp paired-81 
end sequencing on an Illumina HiSeqX sequencing system at t he SNP&SEQ Technology 82 
Platform at Uppsala University (Uppsala, Sweden). The quality of the reads was checked using 83 
FASTQC, and adapters were filtered, and sequences quality trimmed using Trimmomatic v0.39 84 
with default parameters (Bolger et al., 2014). Paired-end reads were then applied to ABySS, a 85 
de novo, parallel  assembler, particularly adapted for paired-end sequence s (Simpson et al., 86 
2009), with default parameter. The resulting genome assemblies ranged from 44.51 Mage bases 87 
(Mb) to 45.71 Mb, with an N50 ranging from 200’252 to 438’025 bp (see summary in Table 1).  88 
 89 
Gene prediction was carried out on contigs with a minimum size of 1000 bp (Table 1) using 90 
Funannotate v1.8.15 (Palmer and Stajich, 2020 ). A total of 11’984 (Nd_iso34), 11’890 91 
(Nd_iso35), and 11’960 (Nd_iso36) high confidence genes were predicted, indicating that ca. 92 
94% of the reference N. ditissima  genome annotation  (Gómez-Cortecero et al., 2015 ) i s 93 
covered by our genome assemblies  (Figure 1 A) . Furthermore, protein size distribution was 94 
consistent over all isolates (Figure 1 B ) further indicating there is no isolate -specific bias in 95 
gene completeness with respect to differences in assembly (Table 1) (Gómez-Cortecero et al., 96 
2015). Finally, analysis of universal fungal single-copy orthologs (BUSCOs) (Kim et al., 2023) 97 
using the Funannotate -annotate pipeline, shows that ca. 97% of the BUSCO terms were 98 
recovered for isolates (Figure 1 C). We thus conclude that the resulting genome assemblies are 99 
of very good quality considering the use of short reads for genome sequencing and assembly. 100 
 101 
Further functional annotation of the predicted proteins was performed using a combination of 102 
Funannotate-annotate v1.8.15, dbCAN (https://bcb.unl.edu/dbCAN2/), signalP v6.0 for signal 103 
peptide prediction (https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/SignalP-6.0/), and targetP  v.2.0 104 
for targeting to the mitochondrion ( https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/TargetP-2.0/) 105 
(see summary in Table 2). Here also, results were consistent among all isolates and within the 106 
expected range with an average of 4038 protein families (Pfams), 559 Carbohydrate-Active 107 
enZymes (CAZymes), 965 putative secreted proteins with a predicted signal peptide (signalP), 108 
and 380 putative mitochondrially targeted proteins (mTP, targetP) (Table 2). We conclude that 109 
overall, the resulting genome annotations have a high level of completeness, which further 110 
demonstrates the quality and relevance of these resources for the study of N. ditissima. 111
